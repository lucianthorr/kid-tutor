Says

Trace: “Trace a lowercase A, three times.”

Copy: “Write a lowercase A, three times.”

From memory: “Write a lowercase A, three times.” (nothing shown)

Done: “Beautiful! Three lowercase A’s.”

Behavior

  • The example sits left of the boxes so a right hand doesn’t cover it while writing.
  • Each box is read by the child’s own recognizer when the pencil pauses, like Spelling. Wrong: the box turns red and clears, “Almost! Try again.” Second miss: the letter appears faded to trace.
  • Help: on level 3 it shows the example; on level 2 it fades the letter into the next empty box.
  • Each letter has its own level. All three right first time, no help → up a level. Needed a trace → down a level. Two clean passes at level 3 → mastered.
  • 6 items per session (about 18 letters to write). Lowercase first, by family: round letters (c a d g o q), tall straight (l t i j k), humps (r n m h b p), then the rest (e s f u v w x y z). Then numbers, then capitals by family (straight, slanted, curved, mixed).
  • Capitals start with no handwriting samples, so a traced or copied letter the recognizer can’t judge yet is saved as a sample (if it’s big enough not to be a scribble). Writing fills in calibration as it goes; the grown-up Calibrate tab stays as it is.
  • Every checked letter becomes a recognizer example, so Writing keeps improving Spelling and Math.

Open questions

  • Later: a gentle tip when a letter floats above the baseline or tall letters don’t reach the top line (never counts as wrong).
  • Later: a “watch me” animation of the stroke order (needs stroke data drawn per letter).
