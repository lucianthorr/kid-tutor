Who am I?

not red not a circle 🐘big
← Finish the gridNext: Session done →

Says

On appear: “Listen to my clues and find me! I'm not red. … I'm not a circle. … I'm big. Who am I?”

Tap a clue: that clue again (“I'm not a circle.”).

Tap a tile: that's the answer. There is no Check button: every puzzle has one answer, so the right or wrong reply comes straight away, and it names the tile.

Right: “You found me! Not red, not a circle, and big. The big blue square!”

Wrong: “The small blue square isn't red and isn't a circle… but I'm big!” (checks each clue against their pick.)

Help: “Listen again. I'm not red. I'm not a circle. I'm big.” and a wrong tile is taken away.

Behavior

  • Each clue is said aloud and shown as a picture chip that can't be mistaken for a tile: a paint drop for a color, the shape in gray (no tile is gray) for a shape, 🐭 or 🐘 for small or big; a dark “no” sign over it for “not”. The clue's words (“not red”, “a star”, “small”) sit under each chip, so a grown-up can follow without the sound, so a non-reader can look back at them. The chip being spoken is outlined in blue.
  • A wrong pick shows why: the clue it breaks turns orange and wiggles, and every tile that clue rules out fades (at any level).
  • Nothing fades by itself. (An earlier version faded tiles as each clue was said; at the easy level that solved the puzzle within seconds of waiting.) The child keeps the clues in mind, with the chips to look back at and tap to hear again.
  • Exactly one answer: every wrong tile breaks exactly one clue, so each clue rules something out and only one tile fits them all. A test checks this for every level.
  • Levels: 4 tiles, 2 clues → 6 tiles, 3 clues (shown) → 8 tiles → 8 tiles, and size can be a “not” too (“I'm not small”). At least one clue always says what it is, never three “not”s.

Open questions

  • At higher levels, should the child be able to tap-and-hold a tile to cross it out themselves?
