# Design principles

A checklist for reviewing any screen. The child-facing rules matter most: the person using those
screens may not read yet, is holding a pencil, and has a short attention span.

## Child-facing screens

1. **No reading required.** Every instruction is spoken when the screen appears, and a big
   replay (speaker) button repeats it. Text on screen is a bonus, never the only way to know what to do.
2. **One thing to do.** Each screen has one obvious main action. Secondary actions are smaller and
   placed away from where the hand rests while writing.
3. **Big targets.** Anything tappable is at least 60 pt (about 1.5 cm), with at least 16 pt between
   targets. Young children tap with the whole finger, and often with the pencil.
4. **Room to write.** Writing boxes are large, with primary-school ruled lines. Children write
   bigger than adults expect.
5. **Kind feedback.** A wrong answer gets a hint and another try, never a buzzer, red X or lost
   points. Right answers get a short, specific celebration ("You spelled *friend*!").
6. **Calm by default.** Motion only where it means something (a reward, a hint). No ads-style
   flashing, no countdown timers, no streak-loss warnings.
7. **Predictable.** The replay button, help button and "next" live in the same place on every
   exercise screen.
8. **Short and finished.** A session has a visible end ("3 more!") and always ends on a success.
9. **Pictures carry meaning.** Use photos and simple pictograms the child recognizes; avoid
   abstract icons for anything the child must understand.
10. **Hand position.** Assume a right-handed child unless set otherwise: keep buttons out from
    under the writing hand (top or left of the writing box).

## Grown-up screens

- Behind a parent PIN; never reachable by accident (no single tap from a child screen).
- Normal iPad conventions are fine: lists, forms, standard controls.
- Lead with what needs attention (struggling words, misread handwriting), then the detail.

## Before calling a wireframe ready

- [ ] What does the app say aloud on this screen? It's written in the notes.
- [ ] Could a child who can't read get through it?
- [ ] Are all child targets 60 pt or bigger?
- [ ] What happens on a wrong answer? On no answer?
- [ ] How does the child get back or get help?
- [ ] Is it linked from `index.html` and from the screens before and after it?
